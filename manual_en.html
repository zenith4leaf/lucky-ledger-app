Lucky Ledger User Guide

Lucky Ledger is a budget app that tracks where your money goes, organized around your cards and bank accounts. In one app you can log spending fast, use an offset to tidy up money friends pay you back, handle credit card bills, carry-over and prepayments, and keep an eye on your card perks.

App version 0.0.30 As of October 5, 2026 (v0.0.30) Android first · same design on iOS

1. Getting started

  1. Choose your language, currency and the day your week starts on. Lucky Ledger supports 7 languages (한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文 · Français · Español · Deutsch) and 20 major currencies. You can change the language and currency later in Settings › General.
  2. Create a 4-digit PIN (required). Turn on fingerprint or face unlock if you like. The lock screen appears every time you open the app, and when you come back after stepping away for a moment.
  3. Add your first asset. Choose from cash, bank account, credit card, debit card or loan. You can't record anything until you have at least one asset.
  4. After a short tutorial, you're ready to go. The tip cards around the app stay hidden once you close them with ✕, and you can bring them back in Settings › Help. The first-use tutorial has 11 steps: 6 steps on the main screens, followed by card perks and linked categories, excluding from the card total, due dates (last day of each month vs. end of the same month), prepayment and carry-over, and money in and out of an asset.
Please remember Your records are stored only on this phone (no sign-in required). If you forget your PIN, you can only reset it with fingerprint or face unlock; if that doesn't work either, you'll have to reset your data. Make a backup every now and then.

3. Adding entries

Tap ＋ and the entry screen slides up from the bottom. Choose Expense / Income / Transfer at the top, type the amount, and tap Save. That's it.

What's on the entry screen

  • Date · time chips: Set to right now by default. Tap to change them.
  • Category badge: Sits in the same row as the date and asset badges. It starts as Choose category; once you pick one, it shows the category name, and you can tap it again to change it (✕ to clear).
  • Asset chip: The asset you used last is selected by default. Tap it to see your assets in the same sections and order as the Assets tab (favorites at the top, hidden assets left out). For a transfer, From → To is shown on one line.
  • Description (store name, etc.): As you type, the app looks for similar past entries and shows suggestions below.
  • Details checkbox: Opens a memo field.
  • Frequent descriptions: Swipe sideways through the descriptions you've used most this year and pick one.
  • Top right: 📋 paste a payment text message you copied · ⭐ load from favorites · 🎤 add by voice · 📷 scan a receipt
  • Who is entering: Once you add family members, a "By ○○" chip appears on the left of the row above the calculator. Tap it to change which family member is entering. When editing, it shows "Editing as ○○" along with the original author (see Family).
  • Buttons at the bottom: Cancel Save

Suggestion badges

When you type a description, category + asset combinations appear as badges in one row next to "✨ Suggested" (most used first, up to 3). If there are many, swipe sideways. Tap a badge to apply it, or pick things yourself.

If there are no similar past entries, the app suggests up to 3 categories that match the description. For example: tuition → Education, wedding gift → Gifts & events, Starbucks → Coffee. Categories you created yourself are suggested too if their names are similar.

Choosing a category

  • The default is the split view: main categories on the left, subcategories on the right. A main category with a number › next to it has subcategories; one without is selected as soon as you tap it. Use the button at the top right to switch to grid view.
  • If the list is long, scroll the whole entry screen up and down to reach the categories at the bottom.
  • Tapping a category only selects it; you save with the Save button. You can pick the category before entering the amount.
  • If you save without choosing, the entry is saved as Uncategorized.
  • The 📝 button at the top right of the category picker takes you straight to Categories to add or edit them. When you come back, the updated list shows right away (this is also available in the category picker for card perk items).

Paying by card

  • Installments (credit cards): Tap the Pay in full chip to choose 2–60 months. The payments are recorded in advance on the same day each month, starting this month. The list shows Installment n/N.
  • Benefit chip: When you pick a category, the linked card perk is selected automatically. See Card perks for details.

Adding by voice 🎤

  • Tap 🎤 at the top right of the entry screen and speak. e.g. "yesterday lunch pizza 9,000 won" → date: yesterday 12:00, amount: 9,000, description: pizza, suggested category: Dining out.
  • The order doesn't matter. "9,000 won pizza yesterday lunch" works the same. Say a card name (e.g. Shinhan Card) and the payment method is chosen too; say something like "salary 3 million won" and it switches to Income.
  • It understands words like today, yesterday, the day before yesterday, tomorrow, "the 3rd", "October 3", "2 pm", breakfast/lunch/dinner, and amounts like "9 thousand" or "12 thousand". It listens in the language you've set (any of the 7 languages).
  • It doesn't save right away. Once the fields are filled in, check them and tap a category to save.
  • Voice input uses your phone's speech recognition (Google). Please allow microphone access the first time.

Scanning a receipt 📷

  • Tap 📷, then Take a photo or Choose from photos.
  • The app reads the receipt and fills in the total, date and time, store name and card type, and puts the purchased items in Details (memo). It suggests a category based on the store and items.
  • It finds the total from words like 합계, 결제금액, TOTAL or 合計, and leaves out tax, cash received and change. It finds the card from the card company name, last digits of the card number, or message keywords.
  • For Korean, Japanese and Chinese receipts, the first scan may take a little longer while the text recognition model downloads (internet required).
  • Take the photo in good light with the whole receipt visible. Again, check the fields and tap a category to save.

Adding from a payment text message

  • Copy a text message from your card company or bank, then tap 📋; the amount, date, store and asset are filled in.
  • You can also share a message from your messaging app to Lucky Ledger without the app opening. You'll just see a notice like "Added to Lucky Ledger · N waiting", and the messages pile up until you open the app, when they all go into the queue at once. If a message with the same content is already recorded or waiting, the app asks "Add it anyway?" when you share it, and asks once more when you record it in the app if it matches a message that's already saved.
  • Messages you share from your messaging app go into a queue, and you can record them one by one by tapping the number badge on ＋. The app lets you know if a message has already been recorded. (Receiving shared messages currently works on Android)
  • For entries saved by pasting or sharing, the app keeps the original message and lets you know if you try to add the same message again.
  • You can view the original message again under Original message in the entry details.

5. Edit · copy · delete

  • Tap an entry to open its details. From there you can Edit, Copy as new entry (with the current time or the original time), Delete, add it to favorites ☆, or make it recurring 🔁.
  • The app always asks you to confirm before deleting, and you can Undo right after deleting.
  • When deleting an installment, choose This month only or Delete all.

Several entries at once

Long-press an entry in the Ledger to enter selection mode. The top shows N selected and the total of the selected amounts, and you can use Delete selected or Edit selected (date, category, asset, description).

You can also long-press to select several entries in the recent entries of asset details. Pressing back while selecting just clears the selection. The same goes for the entries that open when you tap a category in Stats.

6. Offset (getting paid back)

Use this when you paid for a friend's meal with your card and they paid you back later. Instead of counting it as income, it's subtracted from the original expense, so your stats only show what you actually spent.

  1. Open the details of the original expense and tap Offset.
  2. The amount starts as −the remaining amount. If you only got part of it back, just type the number (it automatically becomes a minus amount).
  3. Check the asset that received the money (default: the card's payment account), the date and time (default: the original payment time) and the description, then save.
  • You can offset in several parts (the total can't exceed the original amount).
  • In the list, an offset appears right above the original expense. In the offset's details, View original takes you to the original expense.
  • In the offset list in the original expense's details, you can ✏️ edit or 🗑️ delete each one.
  • Your card bill and card perks don't go down (because the card company still bills the full amount).

7. Ledger tab

  • Date header at the top ‹ Sep 2026 ▾ ›: use the arrows to move, or tap the middle to open a year/month picker (with a Today button inside).
  • Views: Daily (list by date) · Calendar (income and spending totals in each date cell, card due dates marked, long-press a date to add an entry on that date. Dates from the previous and next months appear in light gray; tap one to jump to that month with the date selected) · Monthly (includes weekly totals).
  • Filter: type (expense, income, transfer, offset), asset, category, search words. Active filters show as chips; tap ✕ to clear.
  • Search 🔍: Searches all dates by description, memo and category name, and shows the number of results and their total.
  • Automatic card payments are marked Scheduled before the due date and Done after it.

8. Stats tab

  • Period: Day · Week · Month · Year · Custom. The date header works the same as in the Ledger.
  • The Expense / Income buttons show the total for the period. You can also use filters (asset, category, search words).
  • Spending is calculated after subtracting offsets.
  • Each category has its own color in the charts and lists.

Donut · bar charts

  • Choose Donut (default) or Bar at the top right of the chart.
  • Bar: a vertical bar for each category. Use + Compare month to pick up to 2 months and compare them side by side. Categories that only appear in a comparison month are included too.
  • While comparing, each bar shows which month it is (e.g. Sep Aug) just below the horizontal axis.
  • In bar mode, bar mode and your comparison months stay as they are even when you change the main month at the top.
  • Notice below the chart 🔔: It tells you which category grew the most — compared with the previous period for the donut, or with the comparison month you chose for the bars (e.g. "Dining out spending is up 38% vs Sep").

Category details

Tap a category in the list below, and the matching entries for that period open at the very bottom. Tap it again or tap ✕ to close.

Spending patterns

In the spending stats, tap Spending patterns below the category list, or the 📈 icon next to the filter at the top, to see your spending habits over a month. Use the arrows at the top to change the month; any filters you set in Stats still apply.

  • At a glance: Points out what stands out, in plain sentences. e.g. "Dining out spending is up 38% from last month", "Friday evening spending makes up 46% of all your food spending", "You spend 25% more per day on weekends than weekdays", "You've spent 50,000 more than by this day last month".
  • Spending pace: Shows your running total from the 1st of the month, with this month (dark line) on top of last month (light line). If the dark line is higher, you're spending faster than last month.
  • Merchants you use most: Places you've paid at twice or more, up to 5, by number of visits.
  • Weekday vs weekend: Daily average and total. For the current month, the average is based on the days so far.
  • Spending by time of day: Dawn (0–6) · Morning · Lunch · Afternoon · Evening · Night. The time with the most spending is highlighted.
  • Spending by payment type: Cash/account / Debit card / Credit card.
  • Money you got back (offsets) is subtracted from the original spending.

9. Assets tab

  • Add an asset with + at the top right. Tap 📊 next to it for Asset charts: on top, the year's month-end net worth (switch years), and below, a bar for each asset's balance at the end of the chosen month (◀ ▶ to change the month, or tap a month in the chart above). Debts are shown as red bars.
  • At the top: Net worth on the left, Assets · Debts on the right. This part stays pinned at the top while you scroll the list. Below net worth, the change vs last month is shown in small text (like stock prices: red ▲ for up, blue ▼ for down). The (number) next to the title is how many assets you have.
  • Section totals: The total of each section (group) appears on the right of its title as "Total: amount". Assets with "Include in total" turned off are left out, and sections that are all debts, like cards and loans, show the total you owe in the debt color.
  • Assets are listed by section (cash, bank, cards, loans). Use ⇅ next to a section title to reorder, and ⭐ to move an asset to the Favorites section at the top.
  • Bank accounts show the "expected balance after card bills", debit cards show "paid instantly from the linked account", and credit cards show the upcoming bill and carry-over (see section 10).
  • Asset groups: In Group when adding or editing an asset, pick one of your own groups (e.g. Living expenses, Investments) or create a new one. If you don't pick a group, the asset goes into the default section for its type (Cash & bank / Cards / Loans & other).
  • Manage groups: the 📁 button at the top right of the Assets tab. Drag to change the order of Favorites, the default sections and your own groups, and add, rename or delete your groups. Deleting a group doesn't delete its assets; they go back to the default section.
  • Hidden assets: Hide an asset in its edit screen to keep it out of the list (it still counts in totals). If you have hidden assets, an eye button appears next to Add asset; tap it to show the hidden assets again so you can open them.
  • Card perks this month: How much of each card's benefit limits you've used. Turn on Group view at the top right to group perks with the same name (e.g. Coffee) together (see section 11).

Asset details

  • Changing months: Use ◀ ▶ above the recent entries to change the month (for credit cards, the billing month), and the balance, in, out, card perks and budget at the top all switch to that month. When you look at a past month, the large amount is the balance "as of end of [month]".
  • If you've set budgets per family member, the budget card shows a used/budget gauge for each member along with the shared budget.
  • The recent entries below are grouped by date, just like the Ledger, with each day's income and spending totals next to the date.
  • The large amount on the left is the current balance. The three lines on the right are Prev. balance (+) · In (+) · Out (−) for regular assets, and Prev. used (−) · In (+) · Out (−) for liabilities. For a regular asset, the previous balance plus money in minus money out equals the current balance.
  • In · Out means all the money that came into and went out of that asset. Unlike income and spending in the Ledger, it also includes transfers, card payments and money you got back with an offset. Note that an offset is a negative expense, so it counts as −Out, not as In (e.g. pay 60,000, then offset 40,000 → Out is 20,000). Example: a 30,000 transfer from your bank account to cash is Out for the bank account and In for cash. For a credit card, money paid off with a card payment is In, and card spending is Out. 📈 Asset charts use the same rules.
  • Memo, goal amount · budget gauges (see below).
  • At the top right: 📈 Asset charts (month-end balance trend, monthly in and out), ⭐ favorite, ✏️ edit.

Goal amount · budget gauges

  • Cash · bank · other assets → Goal amount: Enter the amount you want to save in the asset's edit screen, and a gauge fills from 0% → 100% to show how close your current balance is to the goal. Once you pass the goal, you'll see a green bar and 🏆 "Goal reached n%".
  • Cards → Budget (spending limit): Enter a monthly spending limit, and a gauge shows your remaining budget going down from 100% → 0%. It's green while you have plenty left, then turns yellow → orange → red as it shrinks. If you go over, you'll see ⚠️ "Over budget by n%".
  • The gauge appears under the asset in the Assets tab list and in asset details. In the list it's shortened to one line, like "Reached 140% ━━ 7M / 5M". If you don't need gauges, turn off Settings › Display › Show progress gauges to hide them from the list (they still show in asset details).
  • Under the budget in card details, the app tells you how many days are left and how much you can spend per day. e.g. budget 1,500,000, today is the 15th, 1,000,000 left (until the 30th) → "15 days left · spend 66,000 or less a day to stay on budget".

Add · edit · delete

  • Liability: When on, the balance is treated as money you owe and added to your debt total (e.g. money you borrowed, an overdraft account). Liability amounts are shown in the debt color. Credit cards, debit cards and loans always have this on, and it can't be changed. Since a debit card is paid from your bank account the moment you use it, its previous used amount is always 0, and its In (automatic payment) and Out (spending) are the same.
  • Changing the type: Cash, bank account and other assets can be switched between each other in the edit screen (the balance stays the same). Cards and loans can't change type.
  • Turn off Include in total to leave an asset out of your net worth (e.g. a group fund).
  • Enter the last digits and message keywords, and the app can find the asset automatically from payment text messages.
  • A deleted asset disappears from lists and the entry screen, but its name stays on past entries. It can't be restored (unlike hiding).
  • The free version lets you use up to 7 assets. If you're already over that, you can keep using them, but you can't add new ones until you're back under 7.

10. Credit card bills · carry-over · prepayment

The due date, payment account and usage period you set when adding a credit card are a promise: "every month on day N, from this account, I pay for the spending in this period." The app automatically creates your card payments according to that promise.

Choosing a due date: "Last day of each month" and "End of the same month" are different

Due date optionWhat it meansExample
Last day of each monthPays on the last day of every month, for the spending set in the usage period (usually the previous month's spending)Spending Aug 1–31 → paid Sep 30
End of the same monthPays that month's spending from the 1st to the last day at 11:59 PM on the last day of that monthSpending Sep 1–30 → paid Sep 30

In card details, the payment rule appears next to the Card billing title (e.g. Monthly on the 14th, paid from Chase Checking), with the usage period below it in light text in parentheses (e.g. (Usage: 1st of previous month – end of previous month)). Cards paid at the end of the same month show "End of the same month (that month's usage)".

Moving between billing months · unpaid bills

  • The bill box in card details follows the billing month you're viewing: Paid / Scheduled / Unpaid (the due date has passed but it hasn't been paid), prepayments, and carry-over from the previous month.
  • e.g. If it's October and you move to September → you see September's bill amount, and the carry-over is shown as carried over from August.
  • If a bill is unpaid, a Settle button appears in the box. Tap it to mark that bill as paid now.

Turning auto-pay on/off

  • Auto-pay in Edit card › Card billing (on by default).
  • When on: Before the due date, the payment shows in the Ledger in advance as "Scheduled", and on the due date it's paid automatically. If there's still money left after the last payment (an unpaid balance from last month), the app asks "Pay it automatically?" when you open it; tap yes and that amount is paid from the payment account. You're only asked once for the same amount.
  • When off: Even after the due date, nothing is paid automatically and the bill stays Unpaid. When you've actually paid, tap Settle in card details.

From scheduled to paid

  1. When you use the card, the spending goes into the bill that matches the usage period (e.g. the October 14 bill), and the scheduled bill amount updates automatically.
  2. After the due date, the bill is automatically marked paid when you open the app. The money leaves your bank account and your card balance goes down.
  3. Cards paid at the end of the same month (spending from the 1st to the last day, paid at 23:59 that day) are marked paid only after midnight the next day. Spending on the last day is included in the same bill.

Locked bill amounts and carry-over

The moment a bill is paid, its amount is locked. If you later add or delete spending from last month that you forgot, the amount of the finished bill doesn't change; the difference stays on the card as carry-over.

Carry-over = the part of what you owe on the card that isn't in a scheduled bill. It includes spending you entered after the bill was paid, bills you underpaid, and the unpaid balance you entered when you first added the card. There are two ways to clear carry-over.

  • Edit a paid bill: In the details of a paid card payment › Edit, correct the amount that actually left your account and the memo. Any amount you paid less stays as carry-over.
  • Prepayment: Pay it yourself as described below.

Prepayment (paying yourself)

  1. Tap Pay in card details.
  2. Check the amount (default: the next scheduled bill), the account it's paid from, and the date. The description is already filled in with "Prepayment".
  3. If there's carry-over, you'll see a ☑ Include carried over (+30,000) checkbox. Tick it and the amount field is filled with scheduled bill + carry-over. The checkbox only fills in the amount for you; if you type the amount yourself without ticking it, the calculation works exactly the same way.
  • A prepayment pays off carry-over first, and only the amount beyond the carry-over comes off the nearest scheduled bill. Once the carry-over is cleared, it's no longer shown, and any extra appears as "prepayments total".
  • This calculation is always redone regardless of the order you entered things. Even if you prepay first and add forgotten spending from last month (carry-over) later, the prepayment still clears the carry-over first; if you delete that spending, the amount comes off the scheduled bill again. When needed, one prepayment may be shown as two parts: "carry-over cleared" and "prepayment".
  • If you try to pay more than you owe, an alert pops up and it isn't saved. e.g. If the card balance is 147,000, you can pay up to 147,000.
  • If a prepayment pays off this bill completely, you'll see "This bill is fully settled by ○○ in prepayments".
  • The Card billing area in card details shows something like "Already reduced by ₩4,000 in prepayments", so you know how much will actually be paid on the due date.

Recent entries in card details

A credit card's recent entries are shown by billing month. By default only spending (card use) is shown; choose All (incl. payments) above the list to also see card payments and prepayments. The usage period (e.g. Sep 1 – Sep 30) is at the top right, and below it ◀ Oct bill ▶ moves between bills. At first, the nearest scheduled bill opens.

11. Card perks

Manage card perks like "up to 100,000 a month off coffee" in 7 steps.

  1. Prepare categories: In Settings › Categories, first create the categories that match the perk (e.g. Café › Coffee).
  2. Add the card: Add the credit card in the Assets tab and set its due date and usage period.
  3. Perk period: On the card's edit screen, choose "Calendar month" or "Billing period".
  4. Add a benefit limit: Edit card › Benefit limits by category › Add. Enter a name and monthly limit, then tick the linked categories (choosing a main category includes all its subcategories).
  5. Counting toward the card total: Some card companies don't count benefited spending toward your total spending. If you turn on Exclude benefited spending from total on the benefit limit screen, spending up to and including the purchase that reaches the limit is left out of the card total, and spending after that counts again. e.g. limit 600,000, purchases of 500,000 · 200,000 · 100,000 → 500,000 + 200,000 are left out, and 100,000 counts toward the card total. Items with this turned on are marked "Not in total" in card details.
  6. Enter spending: When you use that card and pick a category, the linked perk is selected automatically.
  7. Check: See what's left in "Card perks this month" on the Assets tab and in card details. Tap to see the entries that were counted. In card details, tapping a perk item filters the recent entries to just that perk; tap Total spending to see all entries again.
  8. Once you pass the limit, the used amount in "used / limit" turns green.
  9. Reached: When you fill a limit, it changes to ✅ Reached, and another card with room left in the same category is suggested.

Choosing a perk yourself

  • You can pick a perk yourself with the Benefit chip on the entry screen. Choose "None" to add it only to the card's total spending.
  • If the perk you picked doesn't match the current category, or no category is chosen, the list of categories linked to that perk pops up once more. Pick one and the category changes too, or choose Keep the current category.
  • If you pick a category again after that, the perk is linked automatically again.

Group view

Turn on Group view at the top right of "Card perks this month" in the Assets tab, and perks with the same name (e.g. "Coffee" on several cards) are gathered together. Within each group, cards with the most limit left come first, so you can see right away which card to use next. Your on/off choice is remembered.

12. Recurring · favorites · categories

Recurring entries

Settings › Recurring, or 🔁 in entry details. Daily (you can choose weekdays), weekly, monthly or quarterly, at a set time. When you open the app, any entries due up to today are recorded automatically. You can also pause them or set an end date. The add/edit screen shows "Next entry: date, weekday, time", calculated right away, so you know when the next one will be added.

Favorites

Save entries you use often and load them with ⭐ on the entry screen. Tap an item to just fill in the fields, or tap ⚡ to save it right away without typing anything (when it has an amount).

Categories

Add main categories and subcategories, change names, icons and colors, hide them and reorder them. Categories that are already in use are hidden instead of deleted. Turn off "Subcategories" to enter with main categories only.

Home screen widget

  • Long-press your phone's home screen and add Widgets › Lucky Ledger quick add (Android).
  • The widget shows this month's spending, and the buttons on the right let you add entries right away: Expense · Income · 🎤 voice · 📷 receipt.
  • Tap a button and the app opens straight to that entry screen (voice and receipt start right away). If the PIN lock is on, it appears after you unlock.
  • The spending amount updates whenever your entries change. (iPhone widget coming later)

Moving over from another budgeting app

In Settings › Excel & backup › Restore from another app, you can import another app's backup file. Right now Money Manager (Realbyte) (.mmbak) is supported, and more apps will be added on request.

  1. Make a backup file (.mmbak) in Money Manager and save it on your phone.
  2. In Lucky Ledger, tap Restore from another app → choose Money Manager (Realbyte) and select that file.
  3. When everything has been moved, a completion window appears; close it to return to the Settings screen.
  4. Check the number of assets, categories and entries and the date range, then tap Restore to bring them over. With many entries, this takes a few dozen seconds.
  • All the assets, categories and entries currently in Lucky Ledger will be replaced. Back up first if you need to. (Settings such as language and PIN stay the same.)
  • The free version allows up to 7 assets, so if the backup has more, the app will let you know it can't be moved over.
  • Assets are matched to cash, bank, credit card, debit card, loan or other, and each card's payment account, due date and total spending target come over too. The usage period starts as "1st to end of previous month", so please check it once in the card's edit screen.
  • Categories keep Money Manager's list (main categories and subcategories) as is. Expenses recorded as negative amounts (refunds) become an offset attached to the original expense, so they're subtracted in your stats.
  • Budgets, recurring entries, favorites and photos are not moved over.

14. FAQ

How do I get in touch?
Tap Settings › Help › Contact us and your mail app opens with the recipient (zenith.4leaf@gmail.com) and app version already filled in. If you don't have a mail app, the address is copied instead.
Should I record money a friend paid me back as income?
No. Use an offset on the original expense. If you record it as income, both your spending and your income look bigger than they really are.
Last month's card bill was already paid, and then I added spending I'd forgotten.
The amount of a finished bill doesn't change; the difference stays on the card as carry-over. If you actually paid more, edit the payment entry; if you haven't paid it yet, clear it with a prepayment (tick "Include carried over").
I made a prepayment, but the scheduled bill didn't change.
If there's carry-over, a prepayment pays that off first. Check the "Already reduced by … in prepayments" line in card details and the carry-over shown at the top. If the prepayment is smaller than the carry-over, only the carry-over goes down and the scheduled bill stays the same — that's expected. Prepayments that were recorded incorrectly in older versions are fixed automatically when you open the app on v0.0.9 or later.
Card spending is counted toward the wrong perk.
Check the linked categories of the perk item. If you pick a perk yourself with the Benefit chip when entering, the app asks you to choose one of that perk's categories when the category doesn't match.
The text is too small or too large.
You can choose from 5 sizes in Settings › Display › Display size. Pop-ups like the payment method and category pickers on the entry screen and the calendar scale up and down the same way.
What does it look like on an unfolded foldable or a tablet?
On wide screens, content is shown centered at a comfortable reading width. If you'd rather use the full width of the screen, turn off Settings › Display › Center on wide screens. In the same place, you can also see the screen size and device type the app detected.
